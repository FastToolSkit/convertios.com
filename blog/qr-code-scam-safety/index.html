QR code scams: how to scan safely and protect your customers

A QR code is just a link you cannot read with your eyes. That convenience is exactly what scammers exploit, from stickers on parking meters to emails asking you to "scan to verify". A few habits make scanning safe.

  • CategoryQR & Web

In this article

Why QR codes are attractive to scammers

When you see a written link, you can at least look at it. A QR code hides the address until your phone decodes it, and people tend to trust printed codes in physical places. Scams using QR codes are often called quishing (QR phishing).

Common patterns:

  • Sticker swaps: a fake code stuck over the real one on a parking meter, restaurant table or poster, leading to a look-alike payment page.
  • Phishing emails and letters: "Scan to confirm your account", "scan to receive your refund", often with urgent deadlines. A code in an email also moves you from a protected work computer to a personal phone.
  • Fake delivery and fine notices on paper or cards left at your door.
  • Unexpected codes in public places offering prizes, free Wi-Fi or discounts.

Safe scanning habits

  1. Read the address before opening it. Most phone cameras show the decoded link in a preview banner. Check the domain carefully: misspellings and extra words are warning signs.
  2. Be suspicious of codes that ask for payment or login. For parking, transport and bills, use the official app or type the official website yourself.
  3. Check the physical code. Is it a sticker on top of a printed code? Does it look out of place? Report it to the business or venue.
  4. Do not scan codes from unexpected emails that ask you to verify accounts or reset passwords. Go to the service directly instead.
  5. Watch what the page asks for. A menu does not need your card details; a parking page does not need your email password.
  6. Keep your phone updated so browser and system protections stay current.

If you think you scanned a malicious code

  • Close the page without entering anything.
  • If you entered a password, change it immediately on the real site and enable two-step verification.
  • If you entered card details, contact your bank.
  • Report the code to the business, venue or platform where you found it.

For businesses: keep your codes trustworthy

  • Use your own domain in the code, such as yourbusiness.com/menu, so customers can recognise it in the preview.
  • Print your brand and a clear purpose next to the code: "Scan for our menu".
  • Avoid asking for sensitive data on pages reached by public codes.
  • Inspect codes regularly in public places for stickers placed over them; tamper-evident or laminated placements help.
  • Keep a record of which code goes where, so you can check that each still opens the right page.

A note on what generators can and cannot do
