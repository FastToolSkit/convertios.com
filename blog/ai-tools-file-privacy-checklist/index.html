Before you upload: a privacy checklist for AI and online file tools

Uploading a file to an AI assistant or an online converter is often the quickest way to get a job done. A two-minute check first can stop you sharing far more than the task needs.

Why a checklist helps

AI assistants summarise contracts, explain bank letters, transcribe meetings and read screenshots. Online converters change formats in seconds. Both are genuinely useful, and both may involve sending your file to someone else's computer. The risk is rarely dramatic in a single moment; it is the habit of uploading complete documents when a small part would do.

This checklist is not legal advice. It is a set of questions to ask before you press "upload".

1. Know what the file contains

  • Does it include identity numbers, passport scans, signatures or photos of other people?
  • Does it include financial details, such as account numbers, card numbers or salary?
  • Does it include health, legal or employment information?
  • Is it a work document your employer or client has not cleared for external services?

If the answer to any of these is yes, look for a way to share less, or not to upload at all.

2. Know where the file goes

Find out from the tool's own pages:

  • Is it processed on your device or uploaded? Our comparison of browser-based and server-based tools explains how to tell.
  • How long is it kept? "Deleted after processing", "kept for 30 days" and "stored in your history" are very different.
  • Is it used to train models? Many AI services describe whether user content is used for training and how to opt out, which may differ between free, paid and business accounts.
  • Who else processes it? Some services pass files to third-party providers.

3. Share less

You can often get the same answer with a fraction of the data:

  1. Send only the relevant pages. Extract them with Split PDF, which runs in your browser.
  2. Crop images to the part you need, and remove faces or names that are not relevant.
  3. Remove photo location data. Re-encoding with the image compressor drops original EXIF details such as GPS position; see what photo metadata reveals.
  4. Replace identifiers with placeholders in text you paste: "Client A", "Account ****1234".
  5. Turn a page into a picture of only what matters. Exporting a page with PDF to JPG and cropping it is sometimes simpler than sharing a whole document.

4. Check the account and settings

  • Are you signed in with a personal or a work account? Business plans often have different data terms.
  • Is chat or file history switched on? Can you delete individual items?
  • Have you connected cloud drives or email that the tool can now read?

5. Treat the output carefully too

AI summaries and converted files can contain mistakes. For anything important, compare the result with the original, particularly numbers, names and dates. Our guide to preparing documents for AI assistants explains how file quality affects accuracy.

Quick decision table

FileReasonable approach
Public brochure, published articleUpload is usually fine
Your own CV for feedbackRemove phone number and address if not needed
Bank statement or payslipExtract one page, mask numbers, prefer local tools
ID card or passportAvoid uploading to general AI tools; use official channels
Client or employer documentsCheck policy first; use approved tools only
Medical reportsShare only with trusted, appropriate services
