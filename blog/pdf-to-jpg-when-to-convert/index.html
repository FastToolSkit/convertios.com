PDF to JPG: when turning pages into images makes sense

A PDF is the right container for documents, but sometimes you need a picture: a slide for a social post, a page preview for a website, or an upload form that only accepts images. Here is what changes when a page becomes a JPG.

Good reasons to convert

  • Upload forms that accept only images, for example a profile document field that asks for JPG.
  • Sharing a single page in a chat where a PDF would need opening separately.
  • Previews and thumbnails of a report or brochure for a website or presentation.
  • Social media posts made from a slide or poster that was designed as a PDF.
  • Inserting a page into a document or slide as a picture.

Reasons to keep the PDF

  • Text stops being text. In a JPG, words are pixels: they cannot be searched, selected, copied or read aloud by a screen reader.
  • Multi-page documents become many files. One PDF becomes one image per page.
  • Quality depends on resolution. Zooming into a JPG eventually shows blur, while text in a PDF stays sharp at any zoom.
  • Links and form fields stop working.

If the recipient needs to read, search or print the document, send the PDF. If a form insists on images, check whether it also accepts PDF; many do, even if the label only mentions JPG.

What resolution to expect

PDF pages are measured in points (1/72 inch). When a page is converted, it is rendered to pixels at a chosen scale. The Convertios PDF to JPG Converter renders each page at 1.5× its PDF size, about 108 pixels per inch. An A4 page becomes roughly 890 × 1260 pixels, and a US Letter page about 920 × 1190 pixels.

That is comfortable for viewing on screens, chat apps and web previews. It is not print resolution; for printing, the original PDF is better. For background on pixels and print size, see image DPI and print size.

How to convert with Convertios

  1. Extract the pages you need first if the PDF is long. Split PDF works in your browser; see splitting a PDF and extracting pages.
  2. Open PDF to JPG and choose the file. Pages are rendered in your browser with pdf.js, so the file is not uploaded.
  3. Each page appears with its own Download button and is saved as page-1.jpg, page-2.jpg and so on. Rename them clearly afterwards.

If a form has a strict size limit, reduce the JPG with the Image Compressor, which can aim for a target size in KB.

Tips for readable results

  • Check small text. Open the JPG at 100% and make sure footnotes and table figures are legible.
  • Crop margins if the page will be shown small, so the content fills the image.
  • Mind sensitive data. Converting does not remove anything from the visible page; check for personal details before posting a page publicly.
  • Keep the PDF as the master copy.

JPG or PNG for pages?

JPG suits pages with photos and is usually smaller. Pages that are mostly text, lines and flat colour can look crisper as PNG, because JPG compression can leave faint halos around letters. If a form accepts PNG and the page is a text document, it is worth comparing the two.
