How to remove an image background and get a clean cutout

Automatic background removal works best when the photo makes the subject easy to separate. A few shooting habits and a quick check of the edges turn a decent cutout into a professional one.

  • CategoryImages & File Formats

What you can use a cutout for

A transparent cutout lets you place a subject on any background: a product on white for a marketplace, a portrait on a brand colour, a logo on a slide or a person in a poster. The result is usually saved as a PNG, because PNG supports transparency.

Step 1: shoot with removal in mind

The model separating subject and background relies on visual differences. Help it:

  • Use contrast. A dark product on a light background, or a light product on a darker one.
  • Keep the background plain. A wall or sheet works better than a busy room.
  • Leave space around the subject. Do not crop hands, ears or the edges of a product.
  • Use soft, even light. Harsh shadows can be mistaken for part of the subject.
  • Keep it sharp. Blurry edges produce soft, uncertain masks.

Our explainer on how AI background removal works describes why hair, glass and low contrast are difficult.

Step 2: remove the background in your browser

The Convertios Remove Background tool runs an open-source model inside your browser, so your image is not uploaded. On first use, the browser downloads and caches about 40 MB of model and runtime files; later uses start faster.

  1. Open Remove Background and choose your image.
  2. Wait for the model to process it; older phones take longer than laptops.
  3. Check the preview, then download the transparent PNG.

Step 3: check the edges

Zoom in on the result over a contrasting colour, because problems hide against white:

ProblemWhat to try
Halo of old background colour around hairRetake against a background closer in brightness to the hair, or accept slight softness at small sizes
Parts of the subject missingUse a photo with more contrast between subject and background
Background patches left behindChoose a plainer background; crop the photo closer to the subject before removal
Glass or transparent objects look solidExpected limitation; edit transparency manually in an image editor
Shadow removed and subject floatsAdd a soft shadow in your design tool, or keep a version with background

Keep the original photo so you can try again with a better shot.

Step 4: prepare the file for where it is going

  • Websites and marketplaces: many require a white background rather than transparency. Converting the PNG to JPG fills transparent areas with white; our guide to PNG to JPG and transparency explains what changes.
  • Smaller files: resize with the Image Resizer, choosing PNG or WebP to keep transparency, or compress as PNG or WebP with the image compressor.
  • Print and design: keep the full-size PNG; many design tools let you add a background, shadow or border.

Using cutouts responsibly

Only remove backgrounds from photos you have the right to use, and be careful with photos of other people. Editing context out of an image can change its meaning; for product listings, the item should still be shown accurately.
