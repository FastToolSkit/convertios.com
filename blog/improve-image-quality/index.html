How to improve image quality: what really helps and what cannot be fixed

"Improve quality" can mean sharper, brighter, cleaner or bigger. Each problem has a different cause, and the fix that works for one can make another worse. Start by diagnosing.

  • CategoryAI & Technology
  • Reading time2 min read

In this article

  2. Start with the best source

Diagnose the problem first

Look at the image at 100% zoom and identify what is actually wrong:

What you seeLikely causeBest approach
Soft everywhereCamera shake, missed focus, or enlarged from a small fileRetake if possible; mild sharpening; AI enhancement for display
Blocky squares, halos around edgesHeavy JPG compression, repeated re-savingFind the original; avoid re-compressing; AI can smooth artefacts
Grainy, speckled coloursLow light, high ISO noiseNoise reduction in an editor; AI restoration
Too dark or too brightExposureAdjust brightness, contrast and curves in an editor
Tiny pixel dimensionsThumbnail or screenshot of a photoFind a larger source; AI upscaling for appearance only
Jagged text in a screenshotSaved as JPG or scaledRetake the screenshot and save as PNG

Start with the best source

The single most effective improvement is using a better original:

  • Search your phone, cloud backup or email for the original photo instead of a forwarded copy. Messaging apps usually send compressed versions.
  • Ask the photographer or sender for the full-resolution file.
  • For screenshots, take a new one at the correct size and save it as PNG.

Fixes you can do yourself

  1. Crop and straighten. Removing distracting edges often improves a photo more than any filter.
  2. Adjust light. Most phone gallery apps can brighten shadows, lower highlights and correct colour. Small adjustments look natural; extreme ones create noise.
  3. Sharpen gently. A little sharpening helps after resizing; too much creates bright outlines.
  4. Resize down, not up. A smaller, sharp image looks better than a large, soft one. The Image Resizer runs in your browser.
  5. Choose the right format. Save screenshots and graphics as PNG, photos as JPG or WebP at good quality. The Image Converter switches formats; our JPG, PNG and WebP guide explains the choice.
  6. Stop re-compressing. Each JPG re-save loses a little more. Keep a master copy and export once.

When AI enhancement helps

AI restoration tools can reduce noise and compression artefacts, sharpen details and enlarge small images. They are useful for old family photos, small product images and social media graphics.

Keep in mind that AI adds plausible detail rather than recovering lost information. See AI upscaling versus ordinary resizing for when that matters.

What cannot be fixed

  • Motion blur from a fast-moving subject can be reduced but rarely removed.
  • Completely blown-out highlights (pure white sky) contain no detail to recover.
  • Text or numbers too blurry to read cannot be reconstructed reliably; AI may invent wrong characters.
  • Tiny images printed large will never look like a high-resolution photo.

Quick plan

  1. Find the best original.
  2. Crop, straighten and correct light.
  3. Resize to the size you actually need.
  4. Use AI enhancement only for display, and only on images you are comfortable uploading.
  5. Save in the right format, once.
