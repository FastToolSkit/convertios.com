## Think in shapes first, pixels second

Every social platform shows images in a few standard shapes, called **aspect ratios**: the relationship between width and height. If your image has a different shape, the platform crops it or adds bars. Pixel sizes then decide sharpness.

The most common shapes are:

| Aspect ratio | Shape | Typical uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1:1 | Square | Feed posts, profile pictures |
| 4:5 | Portrait | Feed posts that take more vertical space on phones |
| 9:16 | Tall | Stories, Reels, Shorts, TikTok and other full-screen vertical video or images |
| 16:9 | Wide | YouTube thumbnails, video, many link previews |
| about 1.91:1 | Wide | Link preview images on many networks |
| 3:1 to 4:1 | Very wide | Profile banners and headers |

- **Instagram:** square 1080 × 1080, portrait 1080 × 1350, landscape 1080 × 566, story 1080 × 1920, profile 320 × 320.
- **Facebook:** landscape 1200 × 630, square 1200 × 1200, stories 1080 × 1920, event cover 1920 × 1005, banner 851 × 315.
- **YouTube:** thumbnail 1280 × 720, channel banner 2560 × 1440.
- **X:** post 1200 × 675, header 1500 × 500.
- **LinkedIn:** post 1200 × 627, profile 400 × 400, banner 1584 × 396.
- **TikTok and Snapchat:** vertical 1080 × 1920.
- **Generic ratios:** 1:1, 2:3, 3:2, 4:3, 9:16 and 16:9.

Platforms update their guidance occasionally, so check the platform's own help pages if an upload looks wrong. The aspect ratio is usually what matters most.

## Keep important content in the safe area

Even with the right size, parts of an image can be hidden:

- **Profile pictures** are often shown in a circle. Keep faces and logos centred, with margin all around.
- **Stories and vertical video** overlay the profile name at the top and reply or caption controls at the bottom. Keep text roughly in the middle two thirds of the height.
- **Banners** are cropped differently on phones and desktops, and a profile picture may cover one corner. Put key text near the centre.
- **Thumbnails** are often seen very small. Use few words, large type and strong contrast.

## How to resize one image for many platforms

1. **Start with the largest, best original** you have. Downsizing keeps quality; upsizing blurs.
2. **Compose for the tightest crop.** If the same image will be square and 9:16, keep the subject in the centre so both crops work.
3. **Open the Social Media Image Resizer,** choose the platform groups and select the target sizes.
4. **Preview each size** and download individually or as a ZIP.
5. **For custom sizes,** use the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer), which lets you set exact pixels, crop by dragging a box and choose JPG, PNG or WebP.

## File type and size

- Photos: JPG is accepted everywhere. Platforms recompress uploads anyway, so uploading at the recommended dimensions often matters more than a perfect file.
- Graphics with text: PNG keeps letters crisp before the platform's own compression.
- If an upload is rejected for size, compress it with the [image compressor](tool:compress-image), which can target a size in KB.

## Quick checklist

- Pick the aspect ratio for each placement before designing.
- Keep faces, logos and text away from edges and overlays.
- Export at the recommended pixel size from a larger original.
- Preview on a phone before posting a campaign.
